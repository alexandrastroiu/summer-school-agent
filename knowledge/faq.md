# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is the language spoken in Milan?

The official language is Italian. In tourist areas, hotels and larger restaurants, English is spoken to a reasonable standard. Outside the centre it is less common — knowing a few basic Italian phrases is appreciated. 

## What is the currency?

Italy uses the euro (€). ATMs are widely available throughout Milan — the most cost-effective way to get cash is usually to withdraw from a bank ATM with your debit card. Credit and debit cards are accepted in most restaurants, hotels and shops, though some smaller trattorias and markets still prefer cash. Contactless payments are widely accepted. 

## What is the timezone?
Central European Time (CET).

## Is Milan safe for tourists?
Milan is a safe city for tourists. The main concerns are pickpocketing in busy areas (around the Duomo, on the metro) and unlicensed taxi drivers at airports. Keep bags close, stay aware in crowds, and only use official white taxis.

## Is it safe to use public transport in Milan at night?

Milan's public transport is generally reliable and safe at night.

## What are the most important safety tips for Milan?

- You should know what numbers to call in case of an emergency.
- Learn about the usual scams so you know when to avoid them.
- Check Google reviews before eating or drinking near the major sights, bars and restaurants facing the Duomo or other famous landmarks are often overpriced and disappointing.
- The highest-crime neighborhoods are Quarto Oggiaro, San Siro, and Corvetto, where petty crime and drug-related incidents are most common.
- Milan is generally safe at night, though Via Padova, Baggio, and Viale Monza require extra caution
- Avoid walking alone at night to stay safe, especially in isolated streets. Night buses depart the city center every 30 minutes, offering a reliable way to get home safely.
- Watch out for pickpockets, especially in crowded places like Milano Centrale station, Piazza del Duomo, and on public transport: metro, buses, trams, and trains. Keep your wallet inside your jacket in a zipped pocket. When using public transportation, make sure to keep your bag in front of you.

## How to get to Milan?

When flying to Milan there are three possible Milan airports you can fly into:
- International Airport Malpensa (MXP)
- Airport Linate (LIN)
- Milan Orio al Serio Airport, better known as Milan-Bergamo (BGY)

## How to get around in Milan?

The metro, trams and buses are the most efficient ways to move around Milan. The historic center is also walkable.

## Do you need to tip in Milan?

Tipping is not obligatory in Italy. In restaurants, a coperto (cover charge, €1.50–€3 per person) is standard and already included in the bill. If service has been exceptional, a small tip (€2–5) is appreciated but not expected. In taxis, rounding up the fare is common.

## What is the best time to visit? 

The tourist season in Milan spans around April, the summer months of May-July, and September when people plan their vacations along with their families. Apart from that, the designer and fashion weeks are on around this period, increasing the city's accommodation rate. The travel rates shoot up during this time, and an ordinary ticket may cost you much higher. However, this season is best for sightseeing and outdoor adventures in Milan.

The spring and autumn seasons, which range from March and early April, and October-November respectively, are the best seasons to enjoy Milan's mild pleasant weather. It may be rainy at some times of the day, but one can always compensate for indoor adventures like museum visits, restaurant hopping, etc. Hotel stay prices are relatively lower as not many tourists start going to Milan during these times of the year.

Milan is an absolute no-no during the time of August, as the local people tend to take a vacation during the month and everything remains closed. Besides, Milan's winter is also a very low season as it is always cold and dark. However, if you are an adventure enthusiast, you can well plan to go to some ski resorts nearby and enjoy snowy days.

Museums are one of those places which welcome tourists all year round. However, to beat the crowd and travel in tranquillity, one can prefer all the months except the peak seasons of May-June and September-October, as the lines in the museums are jam-packed.

## How many days in Milan?

Plan at least 2 full days to explore the highlights of Milan. Consider adding one extra day for a trip to Lake Como or Bergamo.

## What to eat in Milan?

You name it, Milan can satisfy it. The city is teeming with restaurants and eateries and choosing what to eat will be difficult: from traditional to ethnic, vegetarian to tapas, gourmet venues to quaint trattorias with red and white tablecloths.

You can't visit Milan without eating the famous risotto alla milanese, with its unique saffron-infused yellow colour,  traditionally served with ossobuco, braised veal shank complete with bone and marrow.
Milano and cotoletta alla milanese are a perfect match. On the bone and cooked in butter, this breaded veal cutlet can also be found ready and steaming in some historic butchers' shops. Meat lovers must try the mondeghili, tasty meatballs made with beef, and the cassoeula, a stew made with pork and vegetables. 


## Do I need a visa?

The documentation required to enter Italy varies according to your country of origin:

- for EU citizens and citizens of countries that have signed the Schengen Agreement, a valid identity card is sufficient as an alternative to a passport
- citizens from non-EU countries may enter Italy with a passport valid for at least three months after the planned date of departure from the Schengen Area

When you will need a visa to enter Italy:

Depending on your country of origin, you may require a visa to enter Italy. You can request a visa from the Italian Embassy or Consulate in your country of residence and it will generally be issued after 90 days. To find out which countries require a visa to enter Italy, please visit esteri.it. On this website, you can enter your nationality, your country of residence, the duration of your stay (less than or more than 90 days) and the reason for your trip. Remember that once you arrive at the border, the authorities may request documentation justifying your reasons for and duration of your stay in Italy.

## What are the emergency numbers?

If you find yourself in serious difficulty and need help, the single number to call for  emergencies in Italy is 112. This is a free number to dial without any prefix: the operator will put you in touch with the most appropriate authority or service.

For needs limited to specific areas, you can call the following numbers:

- State Police: 113 (accidents, thefts, etc.).
- Fire brigade: 115 (fires, weather emergencies).
- Urgent and emergency medical attention: 118 (health-related needs). This number is also relevant for mountain or cave rescue.
- Roadside Assistance: at all hours of the day for motorists in emergency, assistance is provided by the Automobile Club d’Italia (ACI), a federation of 106 provincial Automobile Clubs, which represents and protects the interests of Italian motoring. You can contact them on 803.116 or visit the official ACI website.
- Forest ranger: 1515
- Travel information: 1518
- Sea rescue: 1530
